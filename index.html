Welcome

These are the lecture notes for IMSE 111: Introduction to Industrial and Manufacturing Systems Engineering (3 credits) at North Dakota State University. They’re written for first-year students who want to find out what industrial engineering and manufacturing engineering are, where IMEs work, and how they think. They also teach the software skills engineers use every day.

The notes are released one week at a time and will grow into a full textbook.

About the course

The NDSU catalog lists the course as IMSE 111. Introduction to Industrial and Manufacturing Systems Engineering. 3 Credits. The description reads:

Overview of industrial engineering and manufacturing engineering professional careers and work environments. Basic skill acquisition using computer software tools to solve engineering problems, prepare reports, plan projects, deliver professional presentations, and manage data. (North Dakota State University, n.d.)

That gives the book two threads. The profession covers what IMEs do, where they do it, and why it matters. The toolkit covers spreadsheets, Python notebooks, project plans, reports, and presentations.

What you’ll find in each chapter

Highlighted terms. Key IE vocabulary such as bottleneck or takt time is highlighted. Hover over a term for its definition, or click it to open the glossary.

Stories from the field. Short, sourced stories that show IE at work in real factories, hospitals, and supply chains.

Colab notebooks. Every concept has a hands-on Python notebook you can run in your browser with one click. No installation needed.

In-class activities. Short exercises you can do in pairs or teams during lecture.

Illustrations and diagrams. Process maps, flowcharts, and figures that make systems visible.

References. Every claim is linked to a source. The full list is in References.

How to read these notes

  • Use the sidebar to move between weeks, and the search box (or press /) to find any topic.
  • Turn on dark mode or reader mode with the toggles at the top of the sidebar.
  • Open the Colab notebook in each chapter before class and run it alongside the reading.
  • Read How to Use This Book for a one-page tour of every feature.
